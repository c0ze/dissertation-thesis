\section{Simulation}

This project aims to produce quantum computer simulator that will run on classical computers. Unfortunately, the simulator will be inefficient as it is simulating quantum states on a classical system. Although a quantum computer works on different physical paradigm to a classical computer, quantum computers cannot do anything that classical computers cannot. Classical computers can simulate quantum computers to arbitrary precision by storing vector representations of the quantum states and perform any required rotation upon them. The reason for using quantum computers is merely the increase in efficiency possible over classical computational devices. However, using a classical system to simulate a quantum computer will nullify any efficiency gains due to the extra processing required. It would in fact be much less efficient, as in order to simulate a quantum computer a great deal of information that is implicit in quantum states must be explicitly programmed and manipulated in the classical system. In order to simulate even a small quantum algorithm, consisting of 100 qubits, would require $2^{100}$ (approximately $10^{30}$) complex numbers to represent the quantum state. No existing digital computer, can handle numbers of this magnitude, and that performing any kind of rotation of a vector in a space with a dimension of $10^{30}$ is far beyond the computational capacity of any foreseeable classical computer.

So though a quantum computer can be simulated by a classical computer, the simulation becomes increasingly inefficient as the number of qubits in the simulation increases. Computationally quantum mechanics is very difficult, as the size of the matrices required to store the state of the system grows exponentially with the number of qubits.
